Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about MLB statistics and analytics

What does AVG (Batting Average) mean?

Batting Average (AVG) is one of the most traditional baseball statistics. It's calculated by dividing a player's hits by their at-bats. For example, if a player gets 3 hits in 10 at-bats, their batting average is .300 (3÷10 = 0.300).

A batting average of .300 or higher is considered excellent, .260-.299 is good, and anything below .250 is typically below average for a regular player.

What is ERA (Earned Run Average)?

ERA measures how many earned runs a pitcher allows per nine innings pitched. It's calculated as: (Earned Runs × 9) ÷ Innings Pitched.

An ERA under 3.00 is excellent, 3.00-4.00 is above average, 4.00-5.00 is average, and anything above 5.00 is considered poor for a starting pitcher.

What do RBI and HR stand for?

RBI (Runs Batted In): The number of runs a batter is credited with causing to score. This includes runs scored on hits, sacrifice flies, walks with bases loaded, etc.

HR (Home Runs): The number of times a batter hits the ball over the outfield fence in fair territory, allowing them and all baserunners to score.

What is OBP and SLG?

OBP (On-Base Percentage): Measures how frequently a batter reaches base per plate appearance. It includes hits, walks, and hit-by-pitches. Formula: (H + BB + HBP) ÷ (AB + BB + HBP + SF)

SLG (Slugging Percentage): Measures a batter's power by calculating total bases per at-bat. Formula: Total Bases ÷ At-Bats. A single = 1 base, double = 2, triple = 3, home run = 4.

What are strikeouts and walks?

Strikeout (SO or K): When a batter receives three strikes during their at-bat. Strikes include swinging and missing, not swinging at a pitch in the strike zone, or fouling off pitches (except a foul on the third strike doesn't count unless it's a bunt).

Walk (BB or Base on Balls): When a batter receives four pitches outside the strike zone during their at-bat, they are awarded first base.

What is WAR (Wins Above Replacement)?

WAR is a comprehensive statistic that attempts to capture a player's total value in one number. It estimates how many more wins a player provides to their team compared to a replacement-level player (a readily available minor league or bench player).

WAR Scale:

  • 8+ WAR: MVP caliber season
  • 5-8 WAR: All-Star level
  • 2-5 WAR: Solid starter
  • 0-2 WAR: Role player / bench player
  • Below 0: Replacement level or worse

Note: Different sources (Baseball Reference, FanGraphs) calculate WAR slightly differently.

What is OPS and why is it important?

OPS (On-base Plus Slugging) combines a player's on-base percentage and slugging percentage. It's calculated simply by adding OBP + SLG.

OPS is valuable because it combines a player's ability to get on base with their power hitting, giving a more complete picture of offensive production than batting average alone.

OPS Scale:

  • .900+ : Excellent / All-Star level
  • .800-.900: Above average / Very good
  • .750-.800: Average
  • .700-.750: Below average
  • Below .700: Poor

What is wOBA (Weighted On-Base Average)?

wOBA is similar to on-base percentage, but it weights each outcome (single, double, walk, etc.) according to its actual run value. This makes it more accurate than OBP or OPS at measuring a player's offensive contribution.

The scale is set to look like on-base percentage, where .320 is average and .400 is excellent. wOBA is one of the best single statistics for measuring overall hitting performance.

What is FIP (Fielding Independent Pitching)?

FIP measures what a pitcher's ERA should have been based only on outcomes they can control: strikeouts, walks, hit-by-pitches, and home runs. It removes the influence of fielding and luck on balls in play.

FIP is scaled to ERA, so you can interpret it the same way. If a pitcher's FIP is much lower than their ERA, they may have been unlucky or had poor defensive support. If FIP is higher than ERA, they may have been lucky.

What is WHIP in pitching?

WHIP (Walks and Hits per Inning Pitched) measures how many baserunners a pitcher allows per inning. It's calculated as: (Walks + Hits) ÷ Innings Pitched.

WHIP Scale:

  • Below 1.00: Excellent / Elite
  • 1.00-1.20: Above average
  • 1.20-1.40: Average
  • Above 1.40: Below average

What is ISO (Isolated Power)?

ISO measures a player's raw power by looking at extra-base hits. It's calculated as: SLG - AVG, which essentially shows the extra bases per at-bat beyond singles.

.200+ ISO is considered excellent power, .150-.200 is above average, and below .100 indicates little power.

Yes! Our statistics database includes comprehensive data dating back to the earliest days of professional baseball. However, keep in mind that the game has evolved significantly over time.

Advanced metrics like wRC+ and ERA+ are particularly useful for cross-era comparisons because they're adjusted for league and park factors, making them more comparable across different time periods.

What's the difference between traditional and advanced stats?

Traditional stats are the classic measures that have been used for decades: batting average, home runs, RBI, wins, ERA, etc. They're easy to understand but don't always tell the complete story.

Advanced stats (also called sabermetrics) use more complex calculations to measure player value more accurately. They account for context like ballpark factors, league averages, and defensive support. Examples include WAR, wOBA, FIP, and wRC+.

Both have their place! Traditional stats are great for quick understanding, while advanced stats provide deeper insights for analysis.

What do 2B, 3B, and AB mean?

2B (Doubles): Hits where the batter reaches second base safely.

3B (Triples): Hits where the batter reaches third base safely.

AB (At-Bats): The number of times a batter completes a plate appearance that doesn't result in a walk, hit-by-pitch, sacrifice, or interference.

What is BABIP (Batting Average on Balls In Play)?

BABIP measures how often a ball in play (excluding home runs) falls for a hit. It's calculated as: (H - HR) ÷ (AB - K - HR + SF).

League average BABIP is typically around .300. Unusually high or low BABIP often indicates luck—either good or bad—and players tend to regress toward .300 over time. However, some players consistently maintain higher BABIPs due to speed or hitting approach.

What is a save in baseball?

A save is awarded to a relief pitcher who finishes a game for the winning team under certain conditions:

  • Enters with a lead of no more than three runs and pitches at least one inning, OR
  • Enters with the tying run on base, at bat, or on deck, OR
  • Pitches at least three innings

The pitcher must not be credited with the win to earn a save.

What's the difference between bWAR and fWAR?

bWAR (Baseball Reference WAR) and fWAR (FanGraphs WAR) are two different implementations of the WAR statistic.

The main differences:

  • Defense: They use different defensive metrics (DRS vs UZR)
  • Pitching: bWAR uses actual runs allowed while fWAR uses FIP (outcomes the pitcher controls)
  • League adjustments: Slightly different approaches to league and park factors

Both are valuable. fWAR is better for predicting future performance, while bWAR better captures what actually happened.
